# How to Choose a Mobile Massage Service in Bangkok (Without Getting Burned)

A short, useful version of this post would be: ask a friend who's been to Bangkok recently, get the contact, book the same one.

Most readers don't have that friend. So here's the longer, useful version: a 10-criteria filter that lets you evaluate any Bangkok mobile massage service in about 5 minutes of conversation, before you commit a single baht.

This isn't about finding the *best* service in Bangkok. It's about avoiding the bottom 60% of the market — which is where every bad story you've ever heard about hotel massage in Thailand actually comes from.

## Why This Matters More Than You Think

The mobile massage market in Bangkok has roughly three tiers:

- **Top 10%**: Professional operations, named therapists, transparent pricing, careful screening, repeat customer focus.
- **Middle 30%**: Inconsistent. Sometimes great, sometimes mediocre. Depends entirely on which therapist they dispatch.
- **Bottom 60%**: Anonymous dispatch, vague pricing, untrained or barely trained therapists, sometimes operating in regulatory gray zones that hurt the customer too.

The bottom tier is what most travelers stumble into the first time, because it advertises hardest and has the cheapest entry price. The 10 criteria below are designed to filter it out.

## The 10 Filters

### 1. Are therapists named and profiled?

A reputable service maintains profiles for each therapist — name (or nickname), languages spoken, specialties, and often a photo (which the therapist may or may not allow, depending on local privacy norms — both are legitimate). When you book, you should be able to see who you're getting.

**Red flag:** "We'll send a girl/guy when you book." No name, no profile, no choice. This is dispatch-anonymous, which is the structural definition of the bottom-tier market.

### 2. Is the pricing fixed and visible before booking?

Real services publish pricing per service, per duration, with no surprises. The price quoted in the booking conversation should match the price the therapist asks for at the door.

**Red flag:** Vague pricing ("around 2,000, depends on the therapist"), prices that change after you confirm the booking, or "tip-only" framing where the base service is suspiciously cheap and the actual cost happens at the door.

### 3. Do they confirm in writing?

Reputable services confirm bookings in writing on WhatsApp or Telegram with: time, hotel, service, duration, price, therapist (if requested). You should be able to scroll up and see the agreement.

**Red flag:** Voice-only bookings, bookings made through random unverified Telegram channels, or services that won't put pricing in writing.

### 4. Do they answer questions about discretion clearly?

If you ask, "Can the therapist arrive in plain clothes?" or "How is the booking handled at hotel reception?" — you should get an immediate, professional answer.

**Red flag:** Defensiveness, vagueness, or implications that "discretion" means something other than what you asked.

### 5. Do they ask about *you* before dispatching?

Good services ask: hotel name, your nationality (so they send a therapist with appropriate language skills), preferred time, pressure preference, any injuries. They're matching, not just dispatching.

**Red flag:** They ask only for your hotel and money. No matching = anonymous dispatch.

### 6. Is there a website, app, or established booking system?

Even small operators with 10-15 therapists have at least a basic web presence — a landing page, an app, or a well-maintained social account with consistent branding.

**Red flag:** The entire business runs through a single Telegram channel with no other footprint. This is the bottom-tier signal.

### 7. Are reviews available — and do they read like real customers?

Reviews on Google Maps, TripAdvisor, or expat forum threads (Reddit, expat-specific Singapore forums) are useful. Real reviews mention specific therapists by name, specific hotels, specific service details. Fake reviews are short, generic, and clustered in time.

**Red flag:** Zero reviews anywhere. Or reviews that all read identically and never mention specifics.

### 8. Do they have a clear cancellation/no-show policy?

Professional operators state their policy: "Cancellations up to 1 hour before, full refund. Less than 1 hour, 50% charge. No-show, full charge."

**Red flag:** No policy, or policy that changes case-by-case. Both directions — services that aggressively charge for soft cancellations, or services that have no expectations whatsoever — signal an unprofessional operation.

### 9. Can they handle your hotel?

Most 4- and 5-star hotels in Bangkok permit established mobile services without friction. A good operator knows the policies of major hotels and will tell you up front if there's any issue with yours.

**Red flag:** They claim there's a problem with your hotel but offer to "help you sneak in." A real service either works at a hotel or politely declines — they don't make you complicit in policy violations.

### 10. Do they remember repeat customers?

This is the highest-quality signal and only knowable on your second booking, but worth flagging. A good operator's second-booking conversation should reference your first one: "Welcome back. Same therapist? Same service?" This tells you they're a real CRM-driven small business, not anonymous dispatch.

**Red flag:** Your second booking starts from zero, like they've never heard of you.

## The 5-Minute Test

You don't need to apply all 10 criteria explicitly. In practice, the test is shorter:

1. Open the service's website or main contact channel. Do they pass criteria 1, 2, 6, 7? **(2 minutes)**
2. Send an opening message: "Hi, I'm staying at [hotel] on [date]. Can you give me information about a 90-minute aroma session?" Do they pass criteria 3, 4, 5, 8, 9 in their reply? **(3 minutes)**

If yes to both, you're almost certainly in the top 30% of the market. Book with confidence.

If no — try another service. Bangkok has dozens of operators that pass this filter.

## What "Top 10%" Actually Looks Like

For comparison, here's what a top-tier service interaction reads like in practice (paraphrased from real bookings):

> **You:** Hi, staying at Marriott Marquis Sukhumvit Wed-Fri. Looking for a 90-min aroma Wed night around 11pm. Therapist who speaks decent English. Total cost?
>
> **Service:** Hi, welcome to Bangkok. Yes, we cover Marriott Marquis no problem. For Wed 11pm-12:30am, our recommended therapist is [Name] — fluent English, specializes in aroma and deep tissue, 6 years experience. 90-min aroma session is 2,800 THB inclusive of travel, oils, and sheets. Tips are at your discretion (typical range 300-500 THB).
>
> **Service:** Cancellation policy: up to 1 hour before, no charge. Inside 1 hour, 50% charge. Therapist arrives in plain clothes with a roller bag — looks like flight crew. We don't check in via reception unless your hotel specifically requires it (Marriott Marquis doesn't). Want me to lock the booking in? I'll need your room number when you confirm.

Notice what this exchange does: names the therapist, fixes the price, addresses discretion, addresses cancellations, addresses hotel logistics. The whole thing takes 90 seconds to read. That's what professional looks like.

## A Note on the "Cheapest" Trap

The single biggest mistake first-time travelers make is optimizing for price on the first booking. The 800-1,200 THB market in Bangkok is exactly what you'd expect for that price — anonymous dispatch, untrained therapists, vague booking. The 1,500-2,500 THB tier is where the value-quality ratio is best.

Spending an extra 800-1,200 THB on your first booking is the cheapest insurance you'll buy on the trip. It's the difference between leaving Bangkok thinking "I'd book that again" and leaving thinking "I'll never do that again."

## When to Just Ask the Concierge

Most 4-5 star hotel concierges in Bangkok know which mobile services are reliable. Asking is normal, professional, and not awkward. The wording that works:

> "I'd like to book a mobile massage to my room tonight. Can you recommend a service the hotel works with?"

The concierge will either give you a card or suggest a name. This is one of the highest-trust ways to find a service in Bangkok if you're new.

Note: this doesn't always work — some hotels are deliberately neutral on mobile services to avoid endorsement. But when it works, it works extremely well.

## Final Thought

The mobile massage market in Bangkok rewards the people who do 5 minutes of homework. The filters in this post — written down or applied intuitively — separate the top tier from the noise.

You don't need to find the *best* service. You just need to filter out the bottom 60%. The remaining 40% will all give you a session you're glad you booked.
